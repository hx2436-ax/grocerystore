Rules & assumptions

Each grid cell is 1 m. This single-floor 15×15 store has solid shelves and a solid checkout counter. Movement follows four-way aisles. Items are indivisible and go to their assigned shelf. Every delivery trip starts and ends at (1,1). The objective is minimum walking distance; handling time is excluded.

Up to 14 items: BFS aisle paths, Held–Karp tours and subset partitioning give the global optimum. Larger loads use four packing candidates and minimum-cost insertion, with exact tours per trip but no global guarantee. The baseline is one item per round trip.

SHELF INVENTORY

How customers shop

Customers enter at (1,15), collect their requested quantities, join a first-come checkout queue at (13,14), pay for two seconds, then leave through the entrance. Their shelf order minimizes aisle distance from entry to checkout. Each shelf starts with six items; deliveries add stock. Unavailable items are recorded and skipped.

Solid shelves and the counter block walking. People are visual agents, not moving obstacles: paths may overlap. Pausing customers also pauses their arrivals, browsing and checkout timers. Reset store or changing cargo resets both simulations and restores opening stock.

03 / DISTANCE LAB

One store. Two ways to measure how close you are.

Bonus: a route with multiple stops

Ordered stops are measured from A to B. A shopper preset includes all required shelves, checkout, and the return to the exit; queue movement is excluded.

How the two metrics work

Euclidean distance = √(Δx² + Δy²), ignoring obstacles. Aisle distance = shortest walk along connected four-way aisle edges, avoiding shelves and the counter. Each grid step is 1 m.

Live occupants use their current interpolated position on an aisle edge. Either endpoint of that edge may provide the shortest route. Arriving and exited customers are measured at the entrance staging point, as shown in their status. Updates run about eight times per second.

Close means distance ≤ the chosen threshold, using the same threshold for both metrics. Aisle distance can be greater than the direct distance, but never smaller. A and B label the selected endpoints in 3D.
