Rules & assumptions

Each grid cell is 1 m. This single-floor 15×15 store has solid shelves and a solid checkout counter. Movement follows weighted eight-direction aisles, with no diagonal corner cutting. Items are indivisible and go to their assigned shelf. Every delivery trip starts and ends at (1,1). The objective is minimum static walking distance; people may cause extra walking or waiting. Handling time is excluded.

Up to 14 items: Weighted shortest aisle paths, Held–Karp tours and subset partitioning give the global optimum. Larger loads use four packing candidates and minimum-cost insertion, with exact tours per trip but no global guarantee. The baseline is one item per round trip.

SHELF INVENTORY

How customers shop

Customers enter at (3,1), visit their required shelves, queue at (12,3), pay for three seconds and leave through the adjacent exit (4,1). Arrivals are spaced 12 seconds apart by default; base walking speed is 0.9 m/s. Adjust the controls above to change either. Shelves start with six items and deliveries add stock. Unavailable goods are recorded and skipped.

Eight-direction paths allow diagonal walking without cutting fixture corners. Customers, courier and cashier share a 0.9 m clearance model. Moving corridors are reserved to prevent head-on swaps and crossing diagonals; blocked agents wait or detour. Entry is held if someone is in the doorway. Paused people remain solid obstacles. Reset store restores both simulations. The Pace setting scales walking speed, not arrival gaps or checkout time.

03 / DISTANCE LAB

One store. Two ways to measure how close you are.

Bonus: a route with multiple stops

Ordered stops are measured from A to B. A shopper preset includes all required shelves, checkout, and the return to the exit; queue movement is excluded.

How the two metrics work

Euclidean distance = √(Δx² + Δy²), ignoring obstacles. Aisle distance = shortest walk along connected eight-direction aisle edges, avoiding shelves and the counter. Cardinal steps are 1 m; diagonal steps are √2 m.

Live occupants use their current interpolated position on a cardinal or diagonal aisle edge. Either endpoint of that edge may provide the shortest route. Arriving customers are measured at the entrance, and exited customers at the adjacent exit, as shown in their status. Updates run about eight times per second.

Close means distance ≤ the chosen threshold, using the same threshold for both metrics. Aisle distance can be greater than the direct distance, but never smaller. A and B label the selected endpoints in 3D.

Layout reference

A simplified convenience-store adaptation: rear beverage coolers, central gondola aisles, front checkout and side coffee counter. This is a roofless teaching model, not a surveyed replica.
