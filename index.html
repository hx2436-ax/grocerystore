Rules & assumptions

Each grid cell is 1 m. This single-floor 15×15 store has solid shelves and a solid checkout counter. Movement follows weighted eight-direction aisles, with no diagonal corner cutting. Items are indivisible and go to their assigned shelf. The robot load limit starts at a randomized 24–40 kg; every trip starts and ends at (1,1). The objective is minimum static walking distance; people may cause extra walking or waiting. Handling time is excluded.

Up to 14 items: Weighted shortest aisle paths, Held–Karp tours and subset partitioning give the global optimum. Larger loads use four packing candidates and minimum-cost insertion, with exact tours per trip but no global guarantee. The baseline is one item per round trip.

SHELF INVENTORY

How customers shop

Customers enter at (3,1), visit their required shelves, queue at (12,3), pay for three seconds and leave through the adjacent exit (4,1). Each round randomizes arrival gaps, individual walking speeds, requests and order; customers remain slow. Adjust the controls above to change either. Opening shelf stock and inbound quantities vary each round; robot deliveries add stock. Unavailable goods are recorded and skipped.

Customers walk at arbitrary angles along visible free-floor routes. The delivery robot follows only horizontal and vertical grid edges. Customers, robot and cashier share a 0.9 m clearance model, with slowdown inside 1.5 m. Moving corridors are reserved to prevent head-on swaps and crossing diagonals; blocked agents wait or detour. Entry is held if someone is in the doorway. Paused people remain solid obstacles. Reset store creates a new randomized round. A completed run displays results briefly, then restarts with fresh data. The Pace setting scales walking speed, not arrival gaps or checkout time.

03 / DISTANCE LAB

One store. Two ways to measure how close you are.

Bonus: a route with multiple stops

Ordered stops are measured from A to B. A shopper preset includes all required shelves, checkout, and the return to the exit; queue movement is excluded.

How the two metrics work

Euclidean distance = √(Δx² + Δy²), ignoring obstacles. Aisle distance = shortest walk along visible free-floor segments around inflated fixtures, avoiding shelves and the counter. Customer routes use real segment lengths; robot trip plans use 1 m axial steps.

Live occupants use their actual position anywhere on the current walking segment. Visible free-floor segments connect accessible waypoints; routes are shortest on that visibility graph. Robot plans separately use axial aisle routes. Arriving customers are measured at the entrance, and exited customers at the adjacent exit, as shown in their status. Updates run about eight times per second.

Close means distance ≤ the chosen threshold, using the same threshold for both metrics. Aisle distance can be greater than the direct distance, but never smaller. A and B label the selected endpoints in 3D.

04 / PROXIMITY RULES

See what happens when people or the delivery robot get close.

1 · PERSONAL SPACE

IF another person or robot is within 1.5 m, THEN slow to 45% speed. ELSE continue at normal speed. A step that would breach 0.9 m is stopped or rerouted.

No nearby people
3 · PICKUP RANGE

IF the required shelf is within 0.6 m walking distance and has stock, THEN stop and pick goods. ELSE continue approaching, or skip unavailable goods. The customer and shelf light up green.

No pickup in progress
4 · ROBOT GIVES WAY

IF a 2-second forecast predicts separation below 1.2 m, THEN the robot waits at the next aisle junction or finds a safe axial detour. ELSE continue along the axial route. Purple highlights link robot, customer and predicted meeting point.

Prediction clear

Demonstrations reset the store and stage real agents. Use Reset all to return to a normal run. Customers walk at any angle; the high-capacity robot follows horizontal and vertical aisles.

How the highlights work

Amber means personal-space slowdown. Red means a corridor was blocked before violating the 0.9 m safety clearance. Green identifies pickup within the requested shelf's reachable service zone. Purple identifies predictive robot yielding; a brief afterglow keeps short events visible. The thresholds are simulation parameters.

The forecast assumes current speed and direction for up to two seconds. Robot yielding happens at aisle junctions; the collision controller also protects motion between junctions. After waiting, the robot may take a safe axial detour. People paused mid-pickup still occupy space.
